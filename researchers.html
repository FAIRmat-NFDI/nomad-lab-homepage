For researchers

Make research data reusable.

NOMAD helps researchers explore published results, organize files and structured data, collaborate on projects, and publish citable research. NOMAD Central Platform is the free, public community platform for materials science data and the easiest place to begin.

Why researchers use NOMAD

From research files to reusable results

NOMAD adds structure and context without separating your data from the original files that produced it.

Structured by default
account_tree

Turn files into usable data

NOMAD keeps your original research files and extracts consistent, searchable metadata from recognized formats.

Access & control
lock_open

Work privately, share when ready

Develop a project with collaborators, control access while work is in progress, and decide when the result becomes public.

FAIR research outputs
verified

Publish data others can reuse

Connect files, structured entries, metadata, and an optional DOI in a research output that can be found and cited.

Who this is for

One platform across research projects

Use NOMAD when you generate research data, build on public results, or coordinate work with a team.

science

Researchers producing data

Bring together outputs from simulations, experiments, instruments, and electronic lab notebooks.

travel_explore

Researchers reusing data

Search public results, inspect their provenance, analyze data collections in integrated notebooks, or download them for use in your own research.

groups

Research teams

Organize related work in projects, collaborate before publication, and share completed results.

Data on NOMAD Central Platform

A large & diverse public collection

NOMAD Central Platform brings computational and experimental materials science data into one searchable service. Explore the public collection without an account and inspect both structured results and their original files.

19M+ entries
4.3M+ materials
129.6 TB raw files
150+ supported codes, instruments, and formats
memory

Computational data

Results from electronic-structure, atomistic, molecular dynamics, and workflow codes, normalized for comparison across sources.

biotech

Experimental data

Measurements, microscopy, spectroscopy, synthesis records, and other community-specific data captured with shared and domain-specific schemas.
